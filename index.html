GRAVEYARD
SHIFT

Death is a renewable resource.

Meren of Clan Nel Toth · Recursion, sacrifice and attrition

Concept illustration · View original CMM 685

The business of dying

Most decks treat losing a creature as a setback. Meren treats it as a transaction. The objective is to make that transaction profitable, then repeat it until the table runs out of resources.

Bracket 2 intent — with Bracket 3 potential.

01 / Deploy
Establish mana and useful bodies.
02 / Sacrifice
Convert bodies into cards, mana and experience.
03 / Return
Reuse the right creature for the board.

Working the shift

Early: establish colored mana, stock the graveyard and deploy a cheap outlet or draw engine. Middle: cast Meren with a useful target and preferably a way to extract value before removal. Late: convert recurring bodies into drain, edicts and cards; close rather than prolong a locked board.

Piloting

Keep hands with two or three lands, both colors and early development; three lands is preferable without reliable acceleration. Tutor for the missing role, not a fixed card. Experience counters persist after commander removal. At your end step choose a legal creature target; the experience threshold is checked on resolution.

Weaknesses

Graveyard exile interrupts recursion; preserve hand-based development and removal. Board wipes can remove outlets and draw engines simultaneously. Exile instead of dying avoids death triggers. Creature-light decks reduce the value of edicts. Avoid committing expensive overlapping engines without a path to victory.

Opening hands: keep or send back?

Keep: three lands, both colors, Sakura-Tribe Elder, Viscera Seer, a draw engine and a payoff.

Develop mana first. The outlet can wait if casting it would delay ramp. You have a route to Meren and useful bodies even if she is removed.

Conditional keep: two lands, Arcane Signet and cheap creatures.

Check whether both lands enter untapped when needed and whether you can cast the Signet. A hand relying on drawing a third land is riskier than one with guaranteed development.

Mulligan: two lands, Grave Pact, Butcher of Malakir, Massacre Wurm and expensive recursion.

These are payoffs without infrastructure. A smaller hand with functional mana and early plays is preferable. In multiplayer Commander the first mulligan is free.

Before casting Meren

Ask whether you have a useful graveyard target, a way to earn experience, and enough mana to continue developing if she is removed. Do not delay her automatically: returning a creature to hand can be worthwhile even at zero experience.

Overtime

Ten finite interactions. Examples assume four players and no disruption unless stated.

1. The night shift — Tombstone Stairwell + Zulaport Cutthroat

With Cutthroat in play and five creature cards in your graveyard, each upkeep produces five Tombspawn for you. If all five die at that turn's end step, each opponent loses five life and you gain five. Across four upkeeps that is 20 life per opponent and 20 life gained, assuming the enchantment survives and graveyard counts stay constant. Pay cumulative upkeep on your upkeep; sacrifice outlets can cash in tokens before destruction. Opponents also receive tokens, so plan for their attacks and sacrifice engines.

2. Everybody clocks out — Stairwell + Golbez (Syr Konrad)

Suppose each player has five creature cards in their graveyard. Twenty Tombspawn die at end step, and Konrad deals 20 damage to each opponent if he stays in play. Tokens count as dying creatures but are not creature cards, so their disappearance does not provide an additional graveyard-departure trigger. This is a finite, potentially lethal engine, not an infinite combo. Konrad also counts other creatures dying on either side, qualifying mill events, and creature cards leaving your own graveyard.

3. Double collection — Meren + Twilight Diviner + Gray Merchant

With five experience counters, Diviner and Meren in play, Merchant in your graveyard and no earlier Diviner trigger this turn, return Merchant at your end step. Resolve the Diviner trigger before the first Merchant trigger. Assuming no other black permanents, no removal and standard three opponents, devotion is six (Meren one, Diviner one, two Merchants four); both Merchant triggers drain six each, totaling 12 per opponent and 36 life gained. Check devotion at each resolution. The token copy cannot be reanimated after it dies.

4. The greenhouse — Insidious Roots + Meren

Return one creature card from your graveyard, even to hand, and Roots creates a Plant and grows all Plants. With four separate qualifying departure events and no losses, the Plants are 4/5, 3/4, 2/3 and 1/2. Victimize returning two cards simultaneously triggers Roots only once. All your creature tokens gain the mana ability, but summoning sickness applies. This is compounding board development, not instant unlimited mana.

5. The eviction notice — Grave Pact + Mitotic Slime + free outlet

Sacrifice Slime, then its two 2/2 offspring, then four 1/1 offspring. Seven of your creatures die, creating seven Pact triggers; each other player sacrifices up to seven creatures as those triggers resolve. With Meren present, gain seven experience counters. With Ashnod's Altar as outlet, generate 14 colorless mana. Resolve token generation before attempting subsequent sacrifices. This line can be oppressive; use it to establish a closing window rather than prolong a helpless table.

6. Accelerated experience — Cankerbloom + Meren

With one or more experience counters, activate Cankerbloom's proliferate mode for one mana and its sacrifice. Meren's death trigger goes above the activated ability and resolves first absent interference; proliferating then adds another experience counter. Even starting at zero, this sequence gets you to two if Meren's trigger resolves and you choose yourself. At your end step Cankerbloom can return directly. Repeat only as your mana and end-step opportunities allow.

7. Food chain economics — Ygra + sacrifice outlets

With Ygra, other creatures are Food artifacts. Sacrifice another creature to Viscera Seer or Ashnod's Altar and Ygra gets two counters when that Food goes to the graveyard. The sacrifice also supports Meren and death payoffs. A Food token from elsewhere can grow Ygra even if it is not a creature. Ygra itself is not made Food by its own ability. Do not sacrifice one permanent to pay multiple costs. Artifact removal can now hit other creatures, including opposing ones; Haywire Mite still cannot target creature artifacts because its target must be noncreature.

8. Four shifts, four cards — Dina + free outlet

With Dina, a free sacrifice outlet, and four expendable creatures, sacrifice one during each player's turn to draw four cards over a rotation. Dina's limit resets each turn, not each round. An ordinary death without a sacrifice does not trigger her. If you also control Meren, those four deaths give four experience counters. Save bodies for interaction when needed; do not sacrifice merely to meet a quota.

9. The working capital — Gardenize + Pitiless Plunderer + Mitotic Slime

Seven Slime-family deaths with both engines in play yield seven charge counters and seven Treasures (assuming Plunderer survives). Gardenize supplies seven additional green mana at your next first main phase; Treasures can provide colored mana sooner. Ashnod's Altar as outlet also supplies 14 colorless mana immediately. These are different resources and timings, not interchangeable. Repeating requires another Slime return and a way to sacrifice it.

10. The second interview — Victimize + Twilight Diviner

With Diviner in play and its trigger unused this turn, target Gray Merchant and Eternal Witness with Victimize. Sacrifice a third creature on resolution, return both targets tapped, and choose which one to copy with Diviner. Merchant offers pressure; Witness offers recovery, including the now-resolved Victimize. The copy enters untapped by default. Witness may target Victimize because its ETB trigger is put on the stack after the spell has finished resolving. The line needs new mana and fodder each time, so it is not intrinsically infinite.

Hiring decisions

  • Behind on mana: Buried Alive for Sakura-Tribe Elder, Simulacrum Shaper and Wood Elves; choose according to current experience and available recursion. A graveyard package is not immediate ramp without a return spell or Meren.
  • Under combat pressure: Spore Frog, Plaguecrafter and Shriekmaw (check target restrictions). Prefer immediate hand access with Dina's Guidance if Meren is not ready.
  • Against artifacts or enchantments: Cankerbloom, Haywire Mite and Acidic Slime. Mite exiles itself as a cost, so it is not ordinarily a repeatable Meren target after activation.
  • Ahead and ready to close: Gray Merchant, Golbez and Massacre Wurm. Choose based on devotion, graveyard/death volume and opposing creature counts.
  • Rebuilding: Eternal Witness, Woe Strider and Simulacrum Shaper. Witness can recover a noncreature spell; Strider supplies an outlet and a Goat on entry.

Diabolic Intent and Sidisi find any card; Fauna Shaman finds a creature to hand while discarding a creature; Dina's Guidance finds a creature to hand or graveyard at instant speed; Pattern of Rebirth finds a creature directly to the battlefield when its enchanted creature dies. Use the tutor that fits the destination and timing, not merely the biggest threat.

Choose the destination first

Need an answer now? Diabolic Intent or Sidisi can find a removal spell to hand; Fauna Shaman finds a creature to hand. Check available mana and timing before committing.

Have recursion but no targets? Buried Alive stocks three creatures. Match their mana values to your experience and available return spells.

Have an outlet and an enchanted body? Pattern of Rebirth can put a creature directly onto the battlefield. Account for removal that exiles the enchanted creature instead of killing it.

Facing graveyard hate? Prefer hand or battlefield access and remove the hate before investing in a graveyard package.

Rules of the graveyard

Meren targets at the beginning of your own end step and checks experience on resolution. A creature sacrificed after the target is chosen cannot replace it without changing the target through another effect. Experience belongs to you, and persists after Meren leaves. Token deaths count, but tokens cannot return from a graveyard. If Meren dies simultaneously with other creatures you control, her death-trigger ability sees those other deaths. Graveyard replacement effects that exile cards instead of letting them die can shut off the engine.

No self-contained deterministic two-card infinite loop was identified in this manual review. The ten examples are finite under their stated assumptions. This is not an exhaustive combo search; opposing cards and additional pieces may create other loops. Ygra alone does not provide recurring bodies or free Food activation. Meren normally returns only one card during your end step. Recurring Acidic Slime against lands or repeatedly clearing opposing creatures can create a practical lock even without an infinite loop; avoid using that as the default casual game plan.

Timing checks

Stairwell's token creation occurs at the beginning of each upkeep; destruction occurs at the beginning of each end step and also when Stairwell leaves the battlefield. Its cumulative upkeep is paid only on your upkeep. Tokens dying count for Meren and Syr Konrad, but tokens are not creature cards.

For Twilight Diviner, the qualifying creatures must be other creatures you control; simultaneous returns give one copy, not one per creature. Its once-per-turn limit applies across all qualifying events in that turn.
